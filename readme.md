# Team 4 workflow: 

## The relationship between media framing and audience engagement on 23 YouTube-platformed news videos related to claims on Haitian immigrants by Trump and Vance

This project was developed as part of the workflow assignment for the Digital Humanities course. It examines how the framing of YouTube news video titles relates to audience engagement with disinformation-related content. Focusing on videos discussing claims made by Donald Trump and JD Vance about Haitian immigrants in Springfield, Ohio, we analyze whether titles that explicitly debunk the claims generate different levels of engagement than titles that do not explicitly challenge them.
Drawing on theories of the public sphere, platform capitalism, and affective media, the project approaches YouTube not simply as a neutral platform for news distribution, but as an engagement-driven environment in which visibility and interaction are shaped by algorithmic and emotional dynamics. In particular, the project considers how outrage, controversy, and emotional framing may influence audience interaction through metrics such as views, comments, and likes.
Using a dataset of YouTube news videos and comments related to the controversy, we categorize video titles into two groups: “debunking” and “non-debunking.” We then compare engagement metrics across these categories to explore whether debunking framing affects audience interaction differently from non-debunking framings.
Rather than making generalized claims about misinformation online, this project approaches the dataset as a situated case study that highlights the relationship between media framing, platform logics, and engagement within contemporary digital news environments.
